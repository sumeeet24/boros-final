The House
Page title: The House · Boros Sylvante
Meta description: A study in material, hand and time. An independent Indian House rooted in South Asian craft.
A study in material, hand and time.
Boros Sylvante is an independent Indian House devoted to exceptional natural materials, resolved design, and the knowledge of the hand.
The work begins with material. Its origin. Its texture. Its behaviour. Its memory. Form follows.
The House works across apparel, footwear, bags and accessories, in small numbers, so that the character of the material remains present in the finished piece.
Rooted in India. Informed by the wider traditions of South Asia. Our relationships extend across India, Nepal and Sri Lanka, bringing together distinct fibres, techniques and makers.
We do not reproduce the past. We carry its knowledge forward.
A House in the Making
Boros Sylvante is being built patiently.
First through material. Then through craft. Then through pieces.
Pieces become collections. Collections become an archive. The archive becomes the language of a House.
Born in India. Open to the world.
Our Approach
We are not interested in making more. We are interested in finding better.
Better materials. Exceptional hands. Familiar forms, refined. And time, allowed to do some of the work.
Small quantities are not a device. They are the natural consequence of how we choose to make.
 
The Materials
Page title: The Materials · Boros Sylvante
Meta description: Hemp, nettle, merino, cashmere, pashmina and the rare silks of Assam. Natural fibres of character, chosen for provenance and hand.
What begins the piece.
We begin with what cannot be convincingly imitated. Natural fibres of depth, character and memory.
Hemp. Nettle. Cotton. Linen. Fine wools. Merino. Cashmere. Pashmina. Muga. Eri. Tussar. Mulberry. And, in time, Vicuña.
And considered combinations of them.
Material determines what follows: weight, fall, touch, movement. We choose accordingly.
Each is paired with the hand that understands it best.
Hemp
An ancient fibre. A remarkably contemporary character.
Hemp is central to the material language of Boros Sylvante.
Blended with cashmere, silk, cotton and linen, it becomes a cloth with structure and softness, texture and depth, a dry, cool, living hand.
The result is not a statement about hemp. It is simply beautiful cloth.
Where it belongs: the hills of Nepal and North-East India, where hemp has long been worked by hand.
Nettle
A mountain fibre. Rarely seen, immediately felt.
Himalayan nettle is harvested, stripped, spun and woven by hand. It gives a cloth of natural lustre and quiet strength, with a dry, cool hand that softens with wear.
Where it belongs: the mountain villages of Nepal, and the knowledge of the Himalayan hand.
Cotton
The quiet foundation.
Cool, breathable and honest to the skin. In fine construction, cotton gives cloth clarity and ease, and allows the fibres beside it to speak.
Where it belongs: the handlooms of India and Sri Lanka.
Linen
Irregularity is part of its character.
Structure, breath and a quiet texture that deepens with wear.
Fine Wools
Fineness first.
Fine and superfine wools, chosen for the delicacy of the fibre, its resilience and its clean fall. Woven and knitted for pieces meant to be worn across seasons, and across years.
Merino
Fine, light, exact.
Merino brings softness, drape and an even, disciplined hand. Alone or with silk and cashmere, it gives a cloth composure without weight.
Cashmere
Softness without excess.
Warmth, lightness and a distinctive hand. Used alone or in combination, it changes the character of a cloth without overpowering it.
Where it belongs: the high plateaus of the Himalaya.
Pashmina
Extraordinary softness, handled with restraint.
Approached with care, so that its natural qualities remain at the centre.
Where it belongs: Nepal and the Himalaya, spun and woven by hand on traditional looms.
Silk
Light falls differently on silk.
Not one silk, but many. Each with its own colour, lustre and temperament.
Muga
The golden silk of Assam. Naturally lustrous in a warm, honeyed tone, known for its strength and for deepening with wear.
Where it belongs: Assam, and the weavers of the Brahmaputra valley.
Eri
The soft silk of the North-East. Warm, matte and quietly textured, spun from cocoons after the moth has left.
Where it belongs: Assam and the wider North-East, hand-spun and hand-woven.
Pat
The fine mulberry silk of Assam. Smooth and luminous, woven with patience.
Where it belongs: Sualkuchi and the looms of the Brahmaputra valley.
Tussar
A wild silk with a dry, textured hand and a natural golden-tan colour.
Where it belongs: the forests and weaving villages of eastern and central India.
Mulberry
The finest, smoothest silk, chosen for fluidity and movement. Used where a piece asks for lightness and lustre.
Vicuña
Rare by nature.
Vicuña is not yet part of the collection. It is conceived for a future work: a single masterpiece, to be made when the material and the hand are ready for it.
Its fineness, warmth and touch need no explanation. When it appears, it will be treated with the same restraint as every material in the House.

Provenance
Page title: Provenance · Boros Sylvante
Meta description: Every material begins somewhere. The origins and hands behind Boros Sylvante, across North-East India, Nepal and Sri Lanka.
Every material begins somewhere.
Before a fibre becomes cloth, it has already travelled. Grown, gathered, spun, woven, dyed, shaped, carried through many hands.
These details matter to us.
Our work begins in India and extends across the South Asian craft landscape, including Nepal and Sri Lanka. We build relationships with cultivators, fibre producers, weavers and specialist makers, whose knowledge gives substance to the finished piece.
Provenance is not an appendix to the product. It is part of the product.
India · North-East
Where the House begins.
India offers the foundation of the House's material and craft language: an extraordinary range of fibres, weaving traditions and artisanal knowledge, close to inexhaustible.
Our work begins in the North-East. The golden Muga, the soft Eri and the fine Pat silks of Assam. Weaving that runs from the looms of Sualkuchi to the backstrap looms of the hill communities. Natural dyes, patient colour, patterns carried in memory.
We work within this landscape while developing a language of our own.
Nepal
Mountain knowledge. Exceptional fibres. Patient hands.
Nepal forms part of the House's wider exploration of material and craft.
Himalayan nettle, harvested and spun by hand. Pashmina, spun and woven on traditional looms. Hemp, worked in the hills for generations.
Its relationship with high-altitude fibres and traditional textile practice adds another dimension to our vocabulary.
Sri Lanka
Island traditions. Distinctive craft.
Sri Lanka contributes another strand to the House's South Asian perspective, through its material culture, textile knowledge and artisanal traditions.
Handloom cotton, woven with a light and even hand. Beeralu, the bobbin lace of the southern coast. Cloth coloured by hand.
The intention is not to collect traditions. It is to form meaningful relationships between them.
